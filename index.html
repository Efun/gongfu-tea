Most teas are steeped — leaf in, water on, pour out, repeat. But matcha and hojicha powders don't steep at all: they're whisked into the bowl. Same table, two different crafts.

1

Two Ways to Make Tea

don't mix the methods
🍃

Steeped 冲泡

gongfu leaves · 功夫

Loose or broken leaf into a small vessel, hot water on, a few seconds, pour it all out. Then re-steep the same leaf — six to twenty times, a little longer each round.

Green · White · Yellow · Oolong · Pu-erh · Black · Loose-leaf Hojicha
🥣

Whisked 点茶

powders · 茶粉

Tea is ground to a powder, sifted into a bowl, hot water added, then whisked with a bamboo chasen until frothy. The whole powder is in your bowl — no leaf to re-steep. One prep, one bowl.

Matcha · Hojicha powder
2

The Steeping Pillars

what makes it gongfu
🍃

High Dosage

5–8 g per small gaiwan (≈110 ml). Lots of leaf, little water = depth in every cup.

⏱️

Short & Repeated

Seconds, not minutes. Re-steep the same leaves 6–20 times, lengthening each steep.

🫖

Wake the Leaves

A quick rinse (洗茶) opens the leaf and warms the vessel — essential for pu-erh & aged teas.

⚖️

Pour Evenly

Always through a fair cup (公道杯) so each sip has the same strength, cup to cup.

5

The Steeping Ritual

gongfu, six beats
1

Warm Up 温杯

Flash boiling water through gaiwan, fair cup and cups. Discard. A warm vessel holds heat.

2

Measure & Load 投茶

Weigh the leaf, drop it in. Swirl to wake the dry aroma before the water hits.

3

Rinse 洗茶

Brief splash, pour off fast. Opens the leaf, clears dust. Skip for delicate fresh greens.

4

First Steep 出汤

Pour water, hold the target seconds, then empty completely into the fair cup.

5

Divide & Sip 分茶

Evenly split the fair-cup liquor between cups. Taste, smell, judge strength.

6

Lengthen & Repeat 续水

Add a few seconds next time. The leaf reveals new layers as it unfurls.

6

The Whisking Ritual

powders, three beats
1

Sift 过筛

Sift 1.5–3 g of powder into a warm chawan. Sifting breaks clumps so it whisks smooth, not gritty.

2

Pour 注水

Add 60–80 ml at the right heat — matcha 70–80°C, hojicha 80–90°C. Too hot and matcha turns bitter.

3

Whisk & Sip 点茶

Whisk the chasen briskly in a W or M motion 15–20 s until a fine froth. Drink in a few fresh pulls.

Steeping — temperature is the lever. Delicate young teas want cooler water; roasted oolong & pu-erh want a full boil. Get heat right before touching time.
Steeping — match vessel to tea. A Yixing pot is dedicated to one tea (it drinks in flavor). A neutral gaiwan works for everything and shows true color.
Whisking — water temp is the whole game. Too hot and matcha turns bitter; hojicha forgives you. Sift first, every time, and whisk to a fine froth.
Know your caffeine. Matcha is the strongest cup per spoonful — and it's fully dissolved, so you drink all of it. Hojicha is the gentle, roasty, low-caffeine alternative.
