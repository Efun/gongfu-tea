The Art of Many Infusions

One tea, poured again and again — a little leaf, a small cup, long steeps. The same ritual, tuned differently for every family of tea.

The Four Pillars

what makes it gongfu
🍃

High Dosage

5–8 g per small gaiwan (≈110 ml). Lots of leaf, little water = depth in every cup.

⏱️

Short & Repeated

Seconds, not minutes. Re-steep the same leaves 6–20 times, lengthening each steep.

🫖

Wake the Leaves

A quick rinse (洗茶) opens the leaf and warms the vessel — essential for pu-erh & aged teas.

⚖️

Pour Evenly

Always through a fair cup (公道杯) so each sip has the same strength, cup to cup.

3

The Ritual, Six Beats

repeat per session
1

Warm Up 温杯

Flash boiling water through gaiwan, fair cup and cups. Discard. A warm vessel holds heat.

2

Measure & Load 投茶

Weigh the leaf, drop it in. Swirl to awaken the dry aroma before the water hits.

3

Rinse 洗茶

Brief splash, pour off fast. Opens the leaf, clears dust. Skip for delicate fresh greens.

4

First Steep 出汤

Pour water, hold the target seconds, then empty completely into the fair cup.

5

Divide & Sip 分茶

Evenly split the fair-cup liquor between cups. Taste, smell, judge strength.

6

Lengthen & Repeat 续水

Add a few seconds next time. The leaf reveals new layers as it unfurls.

⚖️Scale1 g accuracy
Temperature is the lever. Delicate young teas want cooler water; roasted oolong & pu-erh want a full boil. Get heat right before touching time.
Match the vessel to the tea. A clay Yixing pot is dedicated to one tea (it drinks in flavor). A neutral gaiwan works for everything and shows true color.
Empty the vessel fully. Leftover liquor keeps extracting and turns the next cup bitter — always drain to the last drop.
When in doubt, go shorter. A weak cup is fixable by re-steeping; a bitter one is gone. You can always add another steep to the same leaf.
功夫 · Gongfu is a practice, not a recipe — tune to your leaf, your water, your mood. Doses & times are starting points for a 110 ml gaiwan · adjust ±20% freely.
